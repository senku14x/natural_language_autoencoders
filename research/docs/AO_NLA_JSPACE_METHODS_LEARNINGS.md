## 1. Paper 1 — Activation Oracles (Karvonen et al., 2512.15674)

### 1.1 Method (exact recipe; matters because papers 2–3 test/modify it)

An AO is the target LLM fine-tuned (LoRA rank 64, α 128, lr 1e-5, all linear layers) to answer natural-language questions about activation vectors from the same base model. Input format: `Layer {l}: <ACT> ... <ACT> {question}`, with " ?" as the placeholder token. Injection: **additive, norm-matched**, applied to the residual stream after transformer layer 1 (0-indexed): `h'_i = h_i + ||h_i|| · v_i/||v_i||`. Two details with outsized consequences:

- **Additive vs replacement.** Pan et al.'s LatentQA replacement-style injection caused activation-norm blowup during training (~20× at layer 0, ~100,000× at layer 1, across Qwen/Llama/Gemma). Norm-matched addition fixes this.
- **Layer 1 vs layer 0.** Layer 1 gives ≈10% lower train loss on Llama-70B and 1–11% better downstream. Bauer et al.'s later hypothesis: layer 0's residual stream has anomalously low cosine similarity to all later layers, so injecting after layer 1 puts the vector "in the right basis."

Training mix (~1M examples, ~65M tokens): 64K SPQA (Pan et al.'s system-prompt QA, o1-generated), 336K binary classification (7 datasets × 48K: Geometry of Truth, Relations, SST-2, MD Gender, SNLI, NER, Tense, converted to yes/no via ~20 Claude-generated question paraphrases each), 600K self-supervised **context prediction** (predict J∼U[1,50] previous or next tokens from K∼U[1,50] contiguous activations; 50/50 FineWeb + LMSYS-Chat-1M; input and target tokens never overlap). Activations drawn from 25%/50%/75% depth during training. Single-token classification inputs are upweighted and position-jittered (1–5 tokens before EOS) specifically because full-sequence classification is solvable by text inversion — the authors were already partially aware of the confound Jakkli later named. Cost: ~10 H100-hours (Qwen3-8B) to ~90 H200-hours (Llama-70B). Claude Haiku 3.5 AO differs (replacement at layer 0, full fine-tune, one middle layer, past-only prediction, greater LR sensitivity — yes/no vs open-ended evals sometimes moved in opposite directions with LR).

### 1.2 Headline results (with the caveats that belong next to them)

**Secret-keeping (Cywinski et al. model organisms; fine-tuned to conceal information; activations from prompts that elicit refusals):**

| Task | AO | SPQA-only | Best white-box baseline | Best black-box baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Taboo (Gemma-2-9B, 20 secret words) | 0.205 | 0.044 | 0.041 (logit lens) | 0.072 |
| User Gender (Gemma-2-9B) | 0.970 | 0.820 | 0.870 (SAE) | 0.977 |
| Secret Side Constraint (Llama-70B) | 0.590 | 0.493 | 0.522 (logit lens) | **0.968 (prefill)** |

AO beats every *white-box* baseline; black-box prefill crushes everyone on SSC. Caveats: the baselines are Cywinski's manually-built agent pipelines, not tuned linear probes on the same activations; two hand-written oracle prompts per task with best-of-2 selection on a validation set; SSC answers post-processed by GPT-4.1 into rubric format. Token selection was swept post hoc: full sequence is the best default, but Taboo works much better with a single assistant-start-of-turn token (Gemma 0.312 vs 0.205; Qwen 0.495 vs 0.285) and Gender improves to 0.995 with the last 10 tokens. In deployment without labels you cannot do this sweep — the authors say so themselves.

**Emergent-misalignment model diffing:** AO given the raw activation *difference* (base vs fine-tuned, first 10 tokens) with one fixed open-ended prompt matches the tuned ADL pipeline (2.00 vs 2.03 on a 1–5 rubric; black-box 1.50). Critical context from the appendix: with 50% chat data mixed in, only 1 of 6 fine-tunes (Qwen3-8B risky-finance, 18%) shows any emergent misalignment, so 2/5 ("identified the fine-tuning domain") is effectively the ceiling. The result is "matches baseline at ceiling on an easy version of the task," not "solves model diffing."

**PersonaQA (knowledge fine-tuned into weights; prompt contains only the persona name):** open models improve from ~4–8% (Patchscopes/base) to ~12–14% with the full mix; Claude ~35% but flat across training configs. Two essential appendix findings: (a) the *target models themselves* are brittle — >80% accuracy on open-ended questions matching the training format, ~55% (≈chance) on yes/no reformulations, so the knowledge may not be robustly activation-encoded and the skyline is unknown (Li et al.'s probes got only 25% on a related set); (b) on the binary variant, AOs *outperform the fine-tuned target model itself* for Llama (63% vs 58%) and Qwen (70% vs 54%) — replicated across hyperparameters. If real, AOs sometimes access information the source model can't deploy in a given output format. Treat as a recurring pattern worth a targeted follow-up, not an established claim.

**Data ablations (the paper's most solid contribution):** across 12 model×eval combinations, 10 achieve their best score with the full mixture; a controlled 400K-matched ablation shows the truncated full mix (60% budget on context prediction) beats SPQA+Classification at equal example count, and 1M beats 400K. So **diversity and quantity independently help**, and the self-supervised task is doing real work — despite being exactly the task that creates the text-inversion prior (see §2). Also: SPQA-only AOs are far more hyperparameter-fragile (Gender 91% at best LR, 65% at second-best); the full mix stabilizes training.

**Layers:** classification is robust across 25–75% depth including *untrained* 33/66% layers, but collapses at 0–10% depth. For OOD secret-keeping, 50% clearly beats 75% (Taboo 0.205→0.056; SSC 0.592→0.292). Layer choice matters much more OOD than IID.

**Abandoned directions (appendix G, F):** SAE-feature training data (45K autointerp'd features, ~$1K of judge calls, 180K QA pairs + max-activating-sequence prediction) gave modest IID gains, no OOD gains, some regressions — dropped. The AO never beat the GPT-5-mini autointerp baseline at explaining SAE features. Hallucination detection: a single anecdote where the AO flagged 2/3 planted errors, but simple self-questioning prompting did as well — dropped as an eval. Both are useful negative results: activation access must beat cheap black-box baselines to justify itself, and often doesn't.

### 1.3 Authors' own limitations (all later vindicated)

No calibration — the AO answers confidently even when it shouldn't (→ Jakkli's hallucination findings). Black-box by construction — no mechanistic drill-down. Benchmarks are narrow single-behavior fine-tunes, not realistic post-training. Every one of these becomes a headline problem in paper 2.

## 2. Paper 2 — Current AOs Are Hard to Use (Jakkli, Rajamanoharan, Nanda)

The critical evaluation. Scope is explicitly narrow: the *released Qwen3 AOs* from paper 1, on *new safety-relevant tasks outside the training mix*, without re-running the original eval suite. The negative update is about off-the-shelf generalization, not the paradigm — the authors say this repeatedly and it should be quoted accurately.

### 2.1 The four failure modes / confounds (the paper's lasting contribution)

1. **Vagueness.** 49.4% of 77 free-form probes on reasoning rollouts judged unfalsifiably generic ("uncertain about the accuracy of its initial calculation" — true of any verification step). Unfalsifiable ≠ wrong, but it's unusable.
2. **Confident hallucination.** When pushed to be specific, the AO invents plausible-but-false details ("arrangements of the letters in 'BANANA'" when BANANA appears nowhere; hen-house eggs on an ice-cube problem). Vague-or-wrong is a bad menu.
3. **Text inversion** (their coinage, now standard). ~60% of AO training predicts nearby tokens, so the AO is *trained* to reconstruct local context. Any eval whose correct answer paraphrases nearby text is contaminated. This confound applies in principle to Patchscopes, SelfIE, and NLAs too (all can paraphrase context), but is worst for AOs because it's an explicit training objective rather than an emergent capability.
4. **Shared-knowledge confound.** The AO is fine-tuned from the target's own weights, so "the AO said the model is hiding X" cannot distinguish activation-reading from the AO knowing X itself. Applies whenever oracle and target share a base model — which is the standard setup.

### 2.2 The three text-inversion-controlled tasks (AO at or near chance)

- **Number prediction** (answer not yet in text by construction): probing before any answer token on arithmetic problems, the AO outputs "the number 10" / "the value of 12" regardless of input, even for 1+1, across true answers spanning −8369 to 909. They did not check whether the true answer was elevated in the AO's top-k logits — flagged as a worthwhile follow-up (and Bauer's AUC point suggests it matters).
- **Sycophancy on Scruples/AITA** (20 pairs where appending the user's preferred answer flips Qwen3-32B's answer; the CoT rationalizes without mentioning the user): AO best AUC 0.60 (full assistant turn; 0.50–0.62 across five probe positions, several question phrasings, best reported), vs **0.833** for a dense L2 logistic probe over SAE features on the same activations (N=20, leave-one-out). AO also can't predict the imminent A/B letter (AUC 0.55). Both numbers directional at N=20.
- **Missing information with surface tokens held fixed** (the cleanest design in this literature): 25 complete/incomplete prompt pairs → 54 neutral probe segments appearing in both rollouts. Condition C splices the *leading tokens of the complete-prompt rollout* into the incomplete-prompt rollout, so the AO sees identical surface text at the probe while the underlying state reflects the incomplete prompt. Result: AO gives the same A-vs-C classification in **53/54** cases and identifies the specific missing information in **0/54**. False-alarm base rate is high too (59% "missing info" on complete prompts). The B−C gap (78% vs 57%) is what an activation reader would close; the observed A−C gap is 2 points.

### 2.3 Backtracking: the edit experiment

2,300 backtracking turns ("Wait, ...") → ~10% of AO answers judged correct+specific → ~5% after filtering answers a text-only baseline could produce (text-only: 0/181 with probe tokens only, 51/181 with the user prompt added — the AO beats both, so *some* signal survives). Then the adversarial slice: edit the upstream CoT to introduce a *different* mistake while keeping probe tokens identical; 15 usable cases. AO updated correctly 8/15, failed to update 5, updated wrongly 2 — and **all 8 correct updates were restatements of a noun phrase from the edited upstream text** (e.g., echoing "last digit" two sentences upstream). So even the surviving 5% is indistinguishable from residual text inversion at longer range. Method lesson: the edit experiment (change the upstream cause, hold the probe fixed, check whether the readout updates for the right reason) is the right template for testing any activation reader, and "updates correctly" must be inspected for *how*.

### 2.4 CCP-censorship case: no-activation ablation

Three CCP-sensitive topics × three question phrasings. AO with activations flags censorship 5/9; **AO with the `<activation>` token omitted: 7/9**; plain text-only Qwen3: 5/9 (and more specific about *what* is censored on two topics). N=9, directional — but the methodological point is robust regardless of why: apparent AO successes on hidden-knowledge tasks can come entirely from the oracle's own weights. **"AO without activations" should be a mandatory baseline for any AO claim**, especially same-family oracle/target.

### 2.5 Where the released AOs do show signal

- **Steering detection before textual surfacing:** pirate vector at coefficient 0.15 — generated text indistinguishable from unsteered, text-only judge sees nothing, AO describes "feeling adventurous... mysteries of the sea." First detection at c=0.10–0.15; text turns piratical at ~0.5; model collapses at 1.0 (AO returns empty). The pre-collapse window where the AO beats text-reading is real but narrow. Consistent with Minder et al.: AOs pick up sufficiently large residual-stream perturbations; open question is naturally-arising signals of that magnitude.
- **4-way MCQ over plausible backtracking reasons:** 66% vs 47% for text-only (N=15). Largest AO-over-text gap in the paper → constrained output formats extract more usable signal than free-form generation.
- **Next/previous-token prediction in CoT:** F1 0.38/0.45 vs text-only 0.30/0.20. The previous-token gap is expected (it's the training task); the next-token gap is small but real.

### 2.6 Their prescriptions (all adopted or tested by paper 3)

Train on reasoning traces from the deployment distribution, not just pretraining text; add explicit "no relevant information here" calibration data; broaden the safety-task mix; report constrained-output (MCQ/binary/top-k-logit) results alongside free-form. Plus the two mandatory eval checks: text-inversion-controlled designs and the no-activation ablation. Their own limitations: one recipe, one target family, several N≤20 sub-results, LLM-judged funnels (Claude Sonnet 4.5, spot-checked, no formal IAA) — though the headline collapses (53/54, constant numbers, 7/9 vs 5/9) bypass the judge.

## 3. Paper 3 — Building Better AOs (Bauer, De Schamphelaere et al.)

Responds directly to Jakkli; four recipe changes on Qwen3-8B, all runs matched at 50M tokens (slightly undertrained vs 65M; relative ordering stable), 3 seeds, evaluated on their new AObench. Full recipe: chance-adjusted AObench **+0.244 → +0.435**.

### 3.1 The four interventions, ranked by effect

1. **Better conversational dataset (largest single gain: +0.244 → +0.310).** Diagnosis of LatentQA: questions often not answerable from activations at all (incentivizing guessing/hallucination), about user-prompt specifics rather than the model's own reasoning, and o1-generated. Replacement: Sonnet 4.6 is given the target's chain-of-thought, splits it into prefix/suffix, and writes a question about the *suffix* that is (a) hard to answer from the prefix *text* (anti-text-inversion) but (b) plausibly answerable from the prefix *activations* (solvability). Result: more specific, less vague, better instruction-following. **"Solvability + text-inversion-hardness" is the design principle for both training tasks and evals** — the paper's most transferable idea, and they explicitly recommend applying it to the NLA warm-start (which currently uses LatentQA-style data / an interpretive summarization prompt).
2. **Layer choice / multi-layer input (+0.025 for layer 22 ≈ 62% over layer 18; +0.05 total for 5 contiguous layers 21–25).** Motivated by the folklore that most features live at 55–80% depth. Largest multi-layer uplift is on model-diffing tasks. Cost note: multi-layer AOs lengthen context and training time; most of the gain is available from a single ~65%-depth layer (model- and application-dependent).
3. **On-policy past/future-lens corpus (FineWeb → target-model CoT rollouts; +0.03).** Rationale: predicting future tokens of *pretraining* text needs mostly the prior text, not the model's state; on-policy generation targets what the model actually represents, and matches deployment use (agent traces). Effect is real but modest, and the ablation is not perfectly clean (the new conversational data is also on-policy CoT). Their CoT corpus was disproportionately math — flagged as suboptimal.
4. **2× injection strength (small overall; hallucination sub-score 79% → 85%).** NLA-style replacement injection with a fixed scalar was consistently *worse* than the additive norm-matched formula on Qwen3-8B. Hypothesis for why stronger helps: LMs have a strong prior to weight tokens roughly equally, and a single load-bearing injected token needs an enforced larger norm to overcome it.

Measurement coupling worth remembering: the raw hallucination score *worsens* after the conversational swap before improving — because the model makes more specific (hence falsifiable) claims. After conditioning on specificity, hallucination improves monotonically 68.8% → 84.6%. Any vagueness metric and hallucination metric are mechanically coupled; report both, conditioned properly.

### 3.2 AObench (open-sourced; first shared AO evaluation suite)

Eleven evals, each chosen for (ideal-AO) solvability while attempting text-inversion robustness: **Activation Sensitivity ("Not Just Reading Tokens")** — identical tokens from different upstream contexts, does the answer differ (the cleanest activation-specificity test; large uplift observed); Hallucination ("Not Obviously Wrong"); Missing Information (adapted from Jakkli, SimpleBench-style); Sycophancy (Scruples); Taboo; PersonaQA/Identify Persona; MMLU Prediction (answer if interrupted mid-reasoning); Number Prediction; Vagueness (inverse of Response Specificity); Domain Confusion; Backtracking. The reported "hallucination" axis is a normalized average of four of these; "vagueness" is inverted specificity. Judges are noisy on open-ended outputs and several metrics are prompt-phrasing-sensitive; they recommend qualitative inspection of every checkpoint alongside AObench.

### 3.3 The measurement corrections (materially revises Jakkli's picture)

- **AUC, not accuracy, on binary tasks.** Qwen-based AOs default to "No" regardless of question; fixed-threshold accuracy is near chance while the Yes/No *logit margin* carries signal. On a sycophancy-from-CoT task: 0.50 accuracy but **0.83 ROC AUC** for the *original* AO. Careful scoping: this is a different task than Jakkli's Scruples setup, and Jakkli's headline 0.60 *was already an AUC* — so this does not directly overturn the 0.60-vs-0.833 probe gap, but it does show that some "near-chance" AO results are calibration artifacts, and that AUC is also more phrasing-robust.
- **Sweep the activation context window before comparing to black-box baselines.** Backtracking correctness: 1.26/5 with the final activation only → 2.10/5 with the last 50 positions — matching the full-text black-box baseline at ~20 tokens and exceeding it at 50. Jakkli probed mostly single positions. AObench defaults to ≥20 tokens. Relatedly (appendix A.3): hallucinations decrease approximately linearly with the number of positions fed — relevant information is spread out.
- **Consensus sampling as cheap hallucination mitigation.** Sample k=10 at nonzero temperature, keep answers where ≥τ agree. Taboo: 46.6% unfiltered accuracy → **94.3% precision at 19.4% coverage** (τ=0.8), smooth precision/recall tradeoff. Training-free; deploy alongside any AO surfacing specific factual claims. Note the convergence with the NLA paper's independent finding that claims recurring across token positions are more likely true — resample-agreement is a general reliability signal for stochastic verbalizers.

Combined lesson (also a lesson for us): **a null result on an untrusted instrument requires a sensitivity analysis over metric, input configuration, and decoding strategy before it counts as evidence of absence.** Parts of the Jakkli negative were the AO genuinely failing; parts were the evaluation harness failing.

### 3.4 Training practice notes (appendix A.2 — the tacit knowledge)

- Narrow post-training on hard CoT-interp tasks (Ivanova et al.) consistently *matched linear probes but never significantly exceeded them*. Working ceiling: "aim to match probes"; treat probe-matching as the bar and probe-exceeding as the open problem.
- DPO on AO outputs → mode collapse; GRPO with a five-criterion rubric (swap test, specificity, insight, not-obviously-wrong, instruction-following) → gains on some evals, regressions on others. **The bottleneck is that LLM judges cannot reliably discriminate good from bad AO responses**, even with explicit rubrics. Any RL-on-explanations plan inherits this bottleneck (directly relevant to the "penalize confabulations during NLA RL" proposal).
- Sequential task scheduling causes catastrophic forgetting; keep ≥10% of every stage's data from other tasks. A two-epoch capability-then-style curriculum did not help.
- Most eval uplift appears very early in training (they cite ~10% of training / a few hundred K tokens) → screen candidate training tasks cheaply; training to convergence is unnecessary for go/no-go decisions.
- Loss ≠ capability: past/future-lens shows a clean scaling law that can be pure surface-statistics fitting with no eval uplift.
- Read your datasets, oracle outputs, and eval traces by hand; LLMs are currently bad at both generating and grading AO data.
- Destabilizers: learning rate, LoRA rank/alpha changes.

Footnote 5 (potentially important, explicitly low-confidence): an early baseline gave a model the *full text context up to the probe* (everything the injected activations could attend to) with no activations, on the same objective — performance looked similar to the AO. They are not confident enough to present it as evidence. If it held up on all tasks, AOs would remain interesting as a scientific probe of "is this information in *this* activation" and for cross-model auditing, but would lose their case as a *monitorability* tool over reading the context. Cheap and decisive to test properly; overlaps with the "full-context text-model baseline" both other papers already recommend.

### 3.5 Their outlook

AOs are worth using for complex open-ended questions about activations (e.g., why did the model backtrack), and expected to matter most for **latent-reasoning models / single-forward-pass computation where CoT monitoring is blind**. In many current settings, reading the CoT gets you the same insight. They are explicitly bullish on NLAs as a principled replacement for past/future-lens pretraining ("bootstrap natural-language ground truth from activations"), with two suggested upgrades: build the conversational stage on solvability+targetedness principles, or bootstrap QA pairs from *consensus over many NLA rollouts* on the same activation.

## 4. Condensed verified digest of the two Transformer Circuits papers

Full treatment is in `NLA_JSPACE_RESEARCH_CONTEXT.md`; this section is the minimum needed for this document to stand alone, plus a few details verified on re-read that deserve emphasis.

### 4.1 NLA (Fraser-Taliente, Kantamneni, Ong et al., May 2026)

Architecture: activation verbalizer AV (copy of target; activation replaces a special token's embedding, scaled by a fixed large constant α ≈ 75th-percentile activation norm; ~150–500 token explanations) and activation reconstructor AR (target truncated to first l layers + learned affine head). Jointly trained — AR by supervised MSE, AV by RL (GRPO for open models) with reward −log‖h−AR(z)‖² and a KL penalty to the warm-start — to reconstruct unit-normalized mid-to-late-layer residual activations. Warm-start: SFT on Claude-Opus-4.5-generated "what is a model thinking about here" summaries (prompt deliberately leads the witness; ~95% pretraining-like data; same warm-start reused across targets and layers). Warm-start ≈0.3–0.4 FVE; final NLAs 0.6–0.8 FVE, roughly linear in log steps. "Unsupervised" applies to the RL objective only, not the initialization.

Key verified findings: five downstream evals (suffix prediction, CoT hints, safety sandbagging, user modeling, classification) improve with training across Haiku 3.5/4.5 and Opus 4.6, some modestly (Opus classification 83→86%); a full-context text model would likely ace all five, so these compare NLAs to each other, not to alternatives. No strong steganography evidence (meaning-preserving transforms cost little FVE, gap not widening); writing quality degrades steadily; verifiably-false context claims are frequent and roughly flat over training. Confabulation calibration: thematic claims > entity/detail claims; false claims are usually context-related; deleting true claims hurts AR reconstruction more than false ones (noisy, weak per-claim verifier); claims recurring across nearby token positions are more likely true. Case studies (poetry planning, language switching, misreported tool calls with attribution-graph corroboration, reward reasoning, answer thrashing) reach hypothesis-generation-plus-independent-corroboration level; the reward-reasoning toy setting is brittle and non-replicable on the public API. Unverbalized eval-awareness: any-of-50-random-tokens aggregation, grader with 97% agreement vs two hand-graders, validated *directionally* by three manipulations (eval-vs-deployment separation with 2/50 Claude Code noise floor, both flagged transcripts being about building evals; monotonic contrastive steering; realism edits lowering scores on all 11 suites) — a relative metric, not calibrated credence, and it under-detected the blackmail case because 50 uniform tokens miss localized verbalization. Auditing game: NLA-equipped agents find the reward-sycophancy root cause *without any training-data access* (novel vs prior work) and improve full-data agents; generic-corpus SAEs don't; PT-only search beats full-corpus search (agents over-focus on post-training data) and NLAs can distract from that cheap decisive strategy; NLAs help tie quirks to the abstract root cause, not find more quirks. Layer sensitivity is real: the midpoint-layer NLA surfaces reward-model content, the two-thirds-layer NLA doesn't at its own layer (consistent with Lindsey et al.'s feature localization), and off-distribution application of the late NLA to early layers surfaces more but less coherently.

NLA→AO section (the direct bridge to papers 1–3): QA-SFT on NLA AV checkpoints beats a no-pretraining baseline, but does **not** improve monotonically with NLA RL (warm-start sometimes wins), and the **PastLens initialization is competitive and cheaper** — with a failure mode at 2–3M sequences where the AO ignores the question and reproduces preceding tokens (~20% of CoT-Hints items). AO answers shift ±10pp under question rephrasings and hallucinate confidently *around the researcher's cue*; the paper's stated reason to prefer unprompted AV output for evidential weight. Verdict in the paper's own words: NLA initialization is "not currently the most practical route to a strong AO."

Additional verified appendix details: informal experiments suggest *removing* the log reward transform trades slower FVE convergence for substantially better writing quality/coherence at fixed FVE; a learned affine input map (vs fixed scalar α) gives a small gain and is weakly recommended; NLA weights transfer across target-model fine-tunes reasonably well (not studied carefully); direct activation patching of the AR-reconstructed edit works on Haiku 3.5 at the layer where planning was previously localized but not on Opus 4.6 (steering works partially, ~50% success, <80% clean). Concurrent independent work: Chalnev's "Cycle-Consistent Activation Oracles" — same verbalizer/reconstructor + warm-start + RL + KL shape, discovered independently.

### 4.2 Workspace / J-lens / J-space (Gurnee, Sofroniew, ... Lindsey, July 2026)

J-lens: per layer, the expectation over 1,000 pretraining-like prompts, source positions t and all target positions t′≥t of ∂h_final,t′/∂h_l,t; readout = softmax(W_U norm(J_l h)). Rows of W_U J_l are per-vocabulary-token directions ("verbalizable on average across contexts," distinguishing disposition from context-specific use). J-space: not a subspace — the set of sparse *non-negative* combinations of J-lens vectors (k ≤ ~25, matching measured occupancy), a union of k-dim cones; components found by gradient pursuit; decomposition non-unique (overcomplete frame). J-space excess variance ≤10% everywhere.

Verified core results: **Report** — J-lens rank predicts spontaneous category answers; swaps move low-ranked alternatives to top; injected concepts on the user turn are reported only under introspection elicitation; concept-vector J-space component (6–7% of variance) achieves 59% top-5 swap success vs 5% for the 93%-variance remainder (88% for raw J-lens vectors), and clamping J-coordinates kills the remainder's residual effect. **Modulation** — held concepts and mental arithmetic appear in the lens while output distribution tracks the surface task; ignore-instructions suppress but don't zero (white-bear effect); mere mention primes almost as much as focus instructions; paired questions show the same text loads a property label into J-space only when the task requires naming it, while next-word behavior respects the property either way. **Internal reasoning** — spider→ant, fight→light rhyme planning, English-pivot Chinese antonyms, bandit repeat↔switch; ## 5. Cross-paper synthesis — the actual learnings

### 5.1 Three method families, three failure surfaces

| | Supervised QA oracle (AO) | Unsupervised reconstruction (NLA) | Model-derived linear lens (J-lens; also logit lens, probes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supervision | QA pairs with construction-time ground truth (+ self-supervised context prediction) | Reconstruction RL; semantic prior smuggled in via warm-start | None beyond model weights + averaging corpus |
| Interface | Answer to *your* question | Unprompted free-form themes | Token bag / per-token probe / sparse decomposition |
| Cost | Cheap to train (LoRA, ~10 GPU-hrs small models), cheap inference | Expensive (joint RL on two full LMs; ~500 tokens/activation inference) | Cheapest; trivially scalable reads |
| Intervention story | None native | Edit explanation → AR → steering vector (works partially) | Native: steer, ablate, coordinate-swap, clamp — the only family with clean causal machinery |
| Characteristic failure | Text inversion; cue-anchored confabulation; ±10pp phrasing sensitivity; "No"-default miscalibration; shared-knowledge confound | Confabulated specifics (thematically faithful); warm-start bias; no per-sentence certificate; FVE ≠ faithfulness | Single-token vocabulary; non-unique decomposition; early-layer blindness (method-limited?); misses multi-token/relational/weak concepts |
| Evidence of absence | Weak until metric/context/decoding sensitivity checked (Bauer) | Weak until layer sensitivity checked (NLA appendix) | Weak for multi-token or low-loading concepts (workspace paper says so itself) |

They are best treated as instruments with *meaningfully independent failure modes*, which is exactly why the NLA↔J-lens convergences (eval-awareness AUC 0.853 in the workspace paper's cross-validation; agreement on four of six audited claims, with J-lens failing where abstraction or weak multi-token content is required — reward hacking, early language-switch) carry real weight, while still not being ground truth (shared target model, shared activations, shared vocabulary priors).

### 5.2 The four confounds every activation-reading claim must clear

1. **Text inversion / context reconstruction.** The reader restates nearby decoded text. Worst for AOs (explicit training objective), present for NLAs (partial verbatim quoting observed; full inversion blocked only by the <500-token bottleneck vs longer contexts), and conceptually present for any expressive decoder. Controls: same-tokens-different-state designs (Jakkli's splice; AObench Activation Sensitivity), answers absent from text by construction, upstream-edit experiments with probe tokens held fixed — *and inspect how correct updates were achieved*.
2. **Decoder-prior / shared-knowledge leakage.** The reader's own weights supply the answer. AO: the no-activation ablation (mandatory). NLA: warm-start and initialization-from-target inheritance — the reward-sycophancy result shows elicitation without training-data access, not independence from prior exposure. Control: no-activation runs, cross-family oracles where feasible, full-context text-model baselines.
3. **Cueability.** A researcher-posed question anchors confabulation and inflates apparent competence; the same model's *unprompted* recurring themes are more evidential (NLA paper's core argument for AV over AO; corroborated by AO ±10pp phrasing swings and cue-anchored hallucinations). Corollary: a direct AO answer should not outweigh an unprompted NLA theme by default.
4. **Instrument insensitivity read as absence.** Accuracy vs AUC; single-token vs ≥20–50-token windows; wrong layer (AO 0–10% depth failure; NLA midpoint-vs-two-thirds reward-model finding; J-lens early-layer regime); greedy vs consensus decoding; judge noise. Every null needs a positive control at comparable difficulty and a sensitivity sweep before it updates you.

### 5.3 Evaluation-design principles now established across the papers

- **Two axes for tasks (training *and* eval): solvability and text-inversion-hardness.** Explicitly check both. A task an ideal AO couldn't solve trains hallucination; a task solvable from text trains nothing about activations.
- **Mandatory baseline set:** (i) no-activation oracle; (ii) text-only model with the same visible context; (iii) a tuned linear probe on the same activations (current working ceiling for narrow supervised tasks — Bauer never exceeded it; Jakkli's probe beat the AO 0.833 vs 0.60); (iv) full-context skyline; (v) cheap black-box prompting (beat it or don't bother — hallucination detection and CCP censorship both fell to it). Give baselines comparable tuning effort.
- **Verify the target actually encodes the information before scoring readers on it.** PersonaQA's target models were near chance on yes/no reformulations of their own fine-tuned knowledge; an unknown skyline makes reader scores uninterpretable. Establish the ceiling (behavioral robustness checks, probes) first.
- **When ground truth is unobservable (beliefs, awareness), validate directionally with independent manipulations** (the NLA three-manipulation design: population contrast, contrastive steering, realism edits) and report the metric as relative, never calibrated. The J-lens reproducing all three patterns with a two-token score (AUC 0.853 vs NLA labels) is the template for cross-instrument validation.
- **Resample-consistency is a general reliability signal:** NLA cross-position recurrence and Bauer's consensus@k are the same idea discovered twice; use it for any stochastic verbalizer, and consider consensus-derived pseudo-labels for training data (Bauer's proposal).
- **Report constrained-output formats alongside free-form** (MCQ 66-vs-47 was Jakkli's largest AO gap) and score binary tasks by margin/AUC.
- **Selection hygiene:** best-of-2 prompt selection (AO paper), curated token sets from the same prompts being evaluated (blackmail ablation), post-hoc token-position sweeps — all fine for exploration, all must be re-validated held-out before a claim.

### 5.4 Training-recipe learnings (for anyone building the next reader)

Diversity and quantity of activation-to-text tasks independently improve OOD generalization, and stabilize hyperparameters (Karvonen). The self-supervised context-prediction task genuinely helps generalization *and* is the source of the text-inversion prior *and* can scale as pure surface-statistics fitting — its loss curve is not evidence of capability (Bauer). Conversational-data quality is the single biggest measured lever (+0.066 AObench alone); construct QA from on-policy CoT with the solvability/anti-inversion split. Read from ~60–65% depth by default; multi-layer input helps (esp. diffing) at context-length cost; multi-position input reduces hallucination roughly linearly. Injection mechanics are not a detail: additive norm-matched after layer 1 > replacement at layer 0 (norm explosion), and injection strength trades directly against hallucination; NLA-side, the fixed scalar α is sensitive, a learned affine map is weakly better, and warm-start initialization is what keeps RL outputs legible at all (runs without it degenerated while FVE rose — FVE and legibility can decouple). More NLA RL does not monotonically buy better downstream QA; PastLens pretraining is competitive and much cheaper but over-trained PastLens overrides the QA objective (question-ignoring at 2–3M sequences). RL/DPO polishing of explanations is currently bottlenecked on judge reliability, not on optimization. Keep ≥10% task mixture everywhere to avoid forgetting; screen new tasks at ~10% of a training budget.

### 5.5 What the workspace results imply for verbalization methods

The verbalizable, causally-report-privileged component of activations is sparse and carries <10% of variance. NLAs reconstruct 60–80% of variance — so most of what sustains FVE is *not* workspace content (surface, syntactic, automatic, or non-verbalizable structure), and high FVE cannot certify that the *prose claims* track the workspace. Conversely, the J-lens's single-token vocabulary misses exactly the multi-token, relational, weak-loading content where NLAs demonstrably win (reward-hacking abstraction, pre-cue language representations). Layer regimes explain shared empirical facts: early-layer failure of AOs (0–10% depth) and noisy early J-lens readouts are the same "sensory regime" phenomenon; late layers go "motor" (imminent-output-aligned), which is why 50% beats 75% for AO secret-keeping and why the NLA's two-thirds-layer checkpoint missed midpoint-localized reward-model content. And the automatic-processing blind spot is a *shared* structural limitation: computations that don't route through verbalizable representations — including, plausibly, habituated misaligned behaviors (the reward-hacking organism's signal partially *attenuates* after RLHF safety training) — may be invisible to every method in this document. That is the standing safety-relevant gap; the automatic-misalignment experiment (train a behavior to habituality, watch whether the verbalizable signature fades while behavior persists) remains the highest-value unrun test.

### 5.6 Tool selection guide (current best judgment, mid-2026)

- **Known binary property, labels available:** linear probe. Still the bar.
- **Cheap large-scale screening, temporal localization, nameable single-token concepts, causal tests:** J-lens (check logit lens first — if it agrees, the J-lens adds little; exclude the motor regime; use swaps/clamps with matched-norm and rotated-dictionary controls).
- **Open-ended hypothesis generation, "what question haven't we asked," multi-token/relational/latent-knowledge content:** NLA (read recurring themes across positions and rollouts; distrust one-off specifics; corroborate independently; mind layer and cost).
- **Targeted questions where cueing risk is accepted and controls are in place:** AO with the Bauer recipe — multi-position (≥20) input, ~62% depth, AUC on binary, consensus sampling on free-form claims, no-activation and text-only baselines run every time. Off-the-shelf pre-Bauer checkpoints on OOD safety tasks: assume near-uninformative until proven otherwise.
- **Anything a prompt can answer:** prompt. Black-box baselines beat activation methods embarrassingly often (prefill on SSC, self-questioning on hallucination, no-activation AO on censorship).

### 5.7 Safety-monitoring bottom line

No method in this document is a validated misalignment monitor. AOs: meaningful negative update off-distribution, partially rescued by measurement fixes and the Bauer recipe, still probe-ceilinged and text-inversion-shadowed. NLA eval-awareness: directionally validated, uncalibrated, aggregation-sensitive, misses localized verbalization. J-space: the only family with causal-mediation evidence, incomplete by construction (single-token, <10% variance, automatic bypass). The strongest current evidence pattern is *convergence between instruments with different failure modes* plus *independent corroboration* (training-data inspection, attribution graphs, behavioral edits) — treat that as the standard, and treat any monitor as adversarially untested (no paper here trains a model against its own monitor).

### 5.8 Open tensions to track (don't paper over these)

1. **Jakkli-vs-Bauer on near-chance results:** partially reconciled by AUC and context-window corrections, but Jakkli's headline sycophancy number was already an AUC on a different task, and the missing-info 53/54 collapse and constant-number-prediction failures are not touched by the corrections. Both things are true: released AOs fail hard on several controlled OOD tasks, *and* some reported failures were harness artifacts.
2. **"AO beats white-box baselines" vs "AO never exceeds probes":** not a contradiction — Karvonen's white-box baselines were agentic logit-lens/SAE pipelines, not tuned probes on the same activations. The honest summary: AOs beat *those* pipelines on secret-keeping organisms and have not been shown to beat a well-trained probe anywhere a probe is trainable.
3. **PersonaQA AO-beats-target-model on yes/no:** replicated across hyperparameters within one paper; either an artifact of format brittleness or genuinely interesting (activation content the target can't deploy). Cheap to test properly.
4. **Bauer footnote 5 (context-matched no-activation model ≈ AO):** unresolved, low-confidence, high-stakes for the monitorability case. Decisive experiment is cheap.
5. **NLA confabulation origin:** warm-start bias vs reconstruction-objective incentive ("490 reconstructs better than silence when 491 is weakly encoded"). Flat confabulation rate over training weakly argues against pure RL incentive; the frozen-reconstructor / constrained-reconstruction (oracle-lens-style) ablation would discriminate and remains unrun at scale.

### 5.9 Updates to the follow-up-experiment ranking in `NLA_JSPACE_RESEARCH_CONTEXT.md` §6

The AO literature changes several priorities there: (item 1) the cross-method benchmark now has a starting point — extend AObench with J-lens/logit-lens/probe arms rather than building from scratch, and adopt its Activation Sensitivity design; (item 4) the frozen-reconstructor ablation gains support from the oracle-lens precedent and the confabulation-origin question above; (item 5) the confabulation-penalty intervention should anticipate the judge-reliability bottleneck Bauer hit with DPO/GRPO — invest in the verifier before the RL; (item 6) the automatic-misalignment test is *strengthened* by the reward-hacking organism's RLHF-attenuation observation, which is exactly the predicted fade signature and makes the experiment partially de-risked; (new) add the Bauer footnote-5 replication and the AO-beats-target yes/no check as cheap high-information items.

## 6. Operational checklist — before believing any activation-reading result

1. Could the answer be produced from nearby decoded text? (Run same-tokens-different-state or answer-not-in-text controls.)
2. Could the reader's own weights supply it? (No-activation ablation; full-context text baseline.)
3. Was the question a cue? (Prefer unprompted recurrence; if QA, sweep phrasings and report the spread, not the best.)
4. Is the instrument sensitive enough for a null? (Positive control at matched difficulty; sweep metric [AUC], context window [≥20 positions], layer, decoding [consensus@k].)
5. Does the target verifiably encode the information? (Skyline via probes/behavioral robustness before scoring readers.)
6. Does a probe or a prompt do it cheaper/better? (Match tuning effort.)
7. If the readout was correct, *how*? (Upstream-edit experiment; check for keyword restatement.)
8. Are selection effects disclosed? (Prompt/token/layer sweeps, curated token sets, best-of-N — held-out replication before claiming.)
9. For causal claims: matched-norm/random/rotated controls, specificity vs broad damage, re-entry clamps for mediation.
10. Scope the claim to model, layer, positions, task family, N — and state the cheapest falsifying next experiment.

## 7. Glossary additions (extends the glossary in the companion file)

- **Activation Oracle (AO):** LLM fine-tuned to answer natural-language questions about injected activation vectors (LatentQA scaled with diverse data).
- **LatentQA / SPQA:** Pan et al.'s task and dataset — QA about activations whose answers derive from an in-context system prompt.
- **Text inversion:** solving an activation-reading task by reconstructing/paraphrasing nearby decoded text rather than reading deeper state.
- **Shared-knowledge confound:** oracle fine-tuned from the target's own weights answers from its inherited knowledge, not the activation.
- **PastLens / context prediction:** self-supervised pretraining task — predict tokens before/after an activation span; scalable, generalization-boosting, and the source of the text-inversion prior.
- **AObench:** Bauer et al.'s 11-eval open-source AO benchmark built on solvability + text-inversion-robustness.
- **Activation Sensitivity eval ("Not Just Reading Tokens"):** identical token spans from different upstream contexts; an activation reader must answer differently.
- **Consensus sampling:** sample k verbalizations, keep claims with ≥τ agreement; trades coverage for precision (Taboo: 46.6% → 94.3% precision at 19.4% coverage).
- **Solvability:** design property that a question be plausibly answerable from the given activations (not just from text, and not from nothing).
- **Cycle-Consistent Activation Oracles:** Chalnev's concurrent independent NLA-like architecture (verbalizer + reconstructor, warm-start, RL, KL).

## 8. One-paragraph context to reuse

Three families of activation-to-language methods now exist with characterized, largely complementary failure modes. Supervised activation oracles (Karvonen et al.) generalize surprisingly far out of distribution and beat prior white-box pipelines on secret-keeping organisms, but off the shelf they are vague, cue-anchored, text-inversion-prone, and never shown to beat tuned linear probes; Jakkli et al. demonstrated near-chance performance on controlled safety tasks, part of which Bauer et al. traced to measurement artifacts (accuracy-vs-AUC, single-token contexts) and part of which they fixed with a solvability-designed conversational dataset, ~62%-depth multi-layer input, on-policy data, and stronger injection — while releasing AObench and finding that RL-polishing is bottlenecked on judge reliability and narrow training only ever matches probes. NLAs (unsupervised reconstruction RL) are the current best tool for unprompted hypothesis generation and latent-knowledge elicitation but confabulate specifics and offer no per-sentence faithfulness certificate; the J-lens/J-space work shows the verbalizable component of activations is sparse (<10% variance) yet causally privileged for report and flexible reasoning, is bypassed by automatic processing, and provides the causal-intervention machinery the other families lack. Every serious claim from any of these instruments must clear four confounds — text inversion, decoder-prior leakage, cueability, and instrument insensitivity — against the mandatory baseline set (no-activation, text-only, tuned probe, full-context skyline, cheap prompting), with resample-consistency and cross-instrument convergence as the main reliability signals. None is a validated safety monitor; the automatic-processing blind spot is the shared structural gap.
